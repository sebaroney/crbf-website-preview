Research

Clinical research is the key to critical change.

Sarcoma is a rare and complex cancer that disproportionately affects children, adolescents and young adults. For too many, treatment options remain limited, therapies can be devastating, and survival outcomes have not improved enough.

Because sarcoma is rare, research funding is often scarce. Yet the need for progress is urgent.

Research is the key to earlier diagnosis, more effective treatments, fewer lifelong side effects and, ultimately, more lives saved.

Every breakthrough begins with an investment. By funding innovative research and strengthening collaboration between Australia's leading scientists and clinicians, we can accelerate discoveries and bring new treatment opportunities to those who desperately need them.

Those diagnosed with sarcoma deserve more than hope. They deserve better treatment options, better outcomes and the chance of a future.

Your support can help make that possible.

Current initiatives

Turning community generosity into research momentum.

$380,000

CaSP-SARC

A dedicated sarcoma stream within the Australian Cancer Screening Program, conducting genomic screening and investigating biomarkers linked to treatment response and resistance.

$300,000

Earlier relapse detection

Research seeking a more reliable way to predict which young osteosarcoma patients are at risk of relapse and create opportunities for earlier intervention.

$312,000

Paediatric preclinical biobank

Supporting a collection of sarcoma models that researchers can use to test targeted therapies and accelerate new treatment discoveries.

$110,000

Tony Denny Foundation scholarship

Backing the next generation of researchers and building specialist capability in sarcoma research.

$100,000

Clinical nurse fellowship

Equipping specialist nurses with advanced clinical, leadership, research and mentoring capability in bone and soft-tissue sarcoma care.

Ongoing

IL-23 sarcoma sub-study

CRBF is a major funding partner in collaborative clinical research evaluating potential new treatment pathways.
