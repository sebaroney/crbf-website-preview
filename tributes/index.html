In memory

Cooper Rice-Brading

In 2016, while undergoing treatment for osteosarcoma, Cooper founded CRBF to change what families facing sarcoma could hope for. He spoke publicly about the urgent need for research and more effective treatments. His vision still guides the Foundation’s work to fund research, raise awareness and support those living with sarcoma and their families.

Read Cooper’s story →

Jack Gibson

Jack drew on his own experience of osteosarcoma to help shape the Foundation’s approach to patient and family support. He understood how much practical help and personal connection could mean during treatment. The programme now bears his name, and his family and friends continue to raise funds so that others can receive the care he helped put in place.

Read about Jack’s legacy of care →

Dr Matthew Fisher

After Matthew was diagnosed with rhabdomyosarcoma in 2020, he and his family formed a close connection with CRBF. The research fund that bears his name has helped support the IL-23 sarcoma clinical study, bringing his family’s commitment to research into work that may improve treatment options for others.

Read about Matthew’s research fund →

Tom Hardyman

Tom is remembered through the Tom Hardyman Memorial Sarcoma Research Fund. The fund joined CRBF and other supporters to help launch Omico Australia’s dedicated sarcoma research programme. That collaboration is helping researchers study the molecular features of sarcoma and explore more personalised treatment options.

Read about the research Tom’s fund helped support →

Andrew Dreverman

Andrew is remembered through the Andrew Dreverman Legacy for Sarcoma Research. Through the Tour de Cure Spring Lunch, supporters helped fund the launch of Omico Australia’s sarcoma programme in his memory. Their contribution brings researchers together to pursue more treatment options for people living with sarcoma.
