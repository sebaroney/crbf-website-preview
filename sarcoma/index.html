Recognising sarcoma and reaching specialist care

Sarcoma is a group of cancers that arise in bone or in soft tissues such as muscle, fat and blood vessels. The first sign depends on where the tumour is growing. A change can warrant assessment even when it causes little or no pain.

Changes to discuss with your GP

Soft tissue. Have a lump assessed promptly if it is growing, feels deep beneath the skin or measures more than 5 cm. A smaller lump that is getting larger also warrants investigation, even if it is painless.

Bone. Persistent, unexplained pain in one area of bone, especially pain at rest or at night, deserves assessment. Local swelling, a new limp, reduced movement or a fracture after a minor injury can also be presenting signs.

These changes have several possible causes. Tell your GP when you first noticed the change and how it has progressed. Arrange a review if it persists or grows after an initial assessment.

Specialist assessment before biopsy

If sarcoma is suspected, Australia’s optimal care pathway recommends referral to a specialist sarcoma multidisciplinary team within two weeks and before a biopsy. The team can select the most informative area to sample and plan the needle path with any future surgery in mind.

Cancer Council · Optimal care pathway for sarcoma

What imaging and biopsy tell the team

An X-ray may be the first investigation for unexplained bone pain. MRI can show the extent of a mass and its relationship to nearby structures. A biopsy, often taken with an image-guided core needle, provides tissue for a pathologist to identify the tumour type. Other scans may be needed to determine whether it has spread.

Cancer Australia · How sarcoma is diagnosed

Planning treatment

The sarcoma team brings together specialists in pathology, imaging, surgery, medical oncology and radiation oncology. Their recommendations take account of the subtype, its location and extent, and what matters to the person receiving care. Depending on those findings, treatment may involve surgery, radiotherapy or medicines such as chemotherapy.
